# Beginner-First Teaching Standard

## Learner profile

The primary learner is a Filipino virtual-assistant aspirant who may know online selling but has **no Amazon, advertising, or PPC background**. The deck must not require the learner to infer business vocabulary, tool behaviour, or the reason for an action.

> The teaching priority is confidence through small, correct steps—not impressive terminology.

## Copy rules

| Design choice | Required practice                                                                                                      |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Language      | Use plain English and short sentences. Prefer a familiar verb such as “check,” “choose,” “add,” or “wait.”             |
| New terms     | Define an Amazon term in the same slide where it first appears. State what it means and why the learner needs it.      |
| Pace          | Teach one new idea per slide. Keep no more than three learner-facing steps or three short comparisons in view at once. |
| Example       | Start with a familiar product-store situation. Then connect it to the Amazon PPC action.                               |
| Direction     | End each instruction slide with a visible “Your next step” or “What to check” prompt.                                  |
| Safety        | Say what **not** to change yet and name the evidence needed before changing it.                                        |

## Preferred wording

| Avoid this                            | Say this instead                                                |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “Diagnose the conversion constraint.” | “Check why shoppers may leave without buying.”                  |
| “Segment by search-term intent.”      | “Group search words by what the shopper is trying to find.”     |
| “Increase bid pressure.”              | “Raise the amount you are willing to pay for one click.”        |
| “Harvest validated queries.”          | “Move a proven search word into its own keyword.”               |
| “Pacing variance.”                    | “Check if the daily budget is spending too fast or too slowly.” |

## Slide rhythm

Each module follows a predictable learner-safe rhythm: **what this is → why it matters → a small example → what to do → what to avoid → proof of readiness**. Formula slides reveal one input at a time and always describe the business meaning of the answer. Assessments use explicit verbs and show the pass target without implying the learner must already know the tool.

## Instructor cue

Before moving on, ask a single retrieval question such as: “In your own words, what would you check first?” If learners cannot answer in simple words, repeat the example before introducing another term.
