# Amazon PH Simulators — Decision Scenario Cards

## How to run the cards

Give one card to a pair or small group. Allow three minutes for silent analysis, five minutes for a defended decision, and two minutes for cross-examination. Require the group to name the observed evidence, the inference, the action, and the next checkpoint. The instructor should withhold the answer until the group states what would change its mind.

## Card 1 — The Invisible Product

**Evidence:** The campaign has 0 impressions and 0 clicks after launch. Inventory is available, but the Buy Box status is unknown.

**Task:** Identify the order of checks before changing the bid. Decide what evidence would justify a bid increase.

**Expected reasoning:** Check Buy Box, inventory, budget, and targeting eligibility before treating the problem as a bid problem. A bid change is a later hypothesis, not the first reflex.

## Card 2 — The Attractive Hook

**Evidence:** The product receives 1,000 impressions and 5 clicks. The main image is generic and the price is above nearby offers.

**Task:** Diagnose the funnel stage and choose the first listing-side intervention.

**Expected reasoning:** CTR is 0.5% from 5 ÷ 1,000. The signal points to the click decision, so improve the listing hook before buying more traffic.

## Card 3 — Clicks Without Orders

**Evidence:** A relevant search term receives 10 clicks, $8 spend, and 0 orders. The listing is missing a key use-case phrase.

**Task:** Decide whether to add a negative immediately or repair relevance first.

**Expected reasoning:** Ten clicks is not automatically enough to declare failure. Because the term is relevant and the listing has a relevance gap, repair the page and define a review checkpoint.

## Card 4 — The Margin Ceiling

**Evidence:** The product has a 30% contribution margin. A campaign produces $20 spend and $100 attributed sales.

**Task:** Calculate ACOS, compare it to break-even, and state the operating implication.

**Expected reasoning:** ACOS is 20% and break-even ACOS is 30%. The campaign is below the margin ceiling, but the instructor should still ask whether the goal is profit, growth, or learning.

## Card 5 — Broad Discovery, Exact Proof

**Evidence:** A broad keyword generates a search term with 3 orders at an acceptable ACOS. A second term is irrelevant and has 15 clicks with no orders.

**Task:** Decide which term moves to Exact and which becomes a negative candidate.

**Expected reasoning:** Move the proven relevant winner to Exact. Investigate and likely negate the irrelevant high-click term, while documenting the reason.

## Card 6 — Budget Pacing

**Evidence:** A profitable campaign spends its full daily budget by 2 p.m. The account has inventory and a positive margin.

**Task:** Decide whether the first action should be a budget increase, bid reduction, or listing change.

**Expected reasoning:** The budget is likely the constraint. A measured budget increase may be justified, but state the guardrail and record the change. Do not change bids and budget at the same time.

## Card 7 — The Weekly Routine

**Evidence:** Three campaign variables were changed on the same day. Performance improved, but the driver is unknown.

**Task:** Explain why this violates the operating method and design the next experiment.

**Expected reasoning:** The account lost causal clarity. Restore a baseline, choose one variable, define the expected effect, and set a review date.

## Card 8 — The Permission Boundary

**Evidence:** A VA wants to pause a core campaign because ACOS increased for one day.

**Task:** Classify the action as independent, notify, or approval-required. Write the message the VA should send.

**Expected reasoning:** Pausing a core campaign is a high-risk structural decision. Stop and ask for approval, provide the evidence, and state the reversible alternative.

## Cross-examination prompts

| Prompt                                    | What it tests                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| What do you actually observe?             | Fact versus inference              |
| What would you change first?              | Prioritization                     |
| What would you leave unchanged?           | Restraint and experimental control |
| What would make you reverse the decision? | Risk and reversibility             |
| What belongs in the change log?           | Operational discipline             |
