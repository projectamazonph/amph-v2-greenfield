# Module 3 Field Handout — Campaign Structure

## The job

By the end of this module, you should be able to **Use campaign, ad-group, and targeting hierarchy; apply naming conventions; distinguish SP, SB, and SD.** The required practice handoff is **Campaign Architect** (`campaign-architect.html`), with a target of **75% simulator / 70% quiz**.

> **Operating rule:** Structure exists to preserve control, reading clarity, and a clean next action.

## What to look for

- **1.** One purpose per campaign
- **2.** Targeting and match-type separation
- **3.** Traceable naming convention
- **4.** Evidence routing from search term to action

## Worked thinking

A campaign that mixes discovery, harvesting, and defense hides the cause of performance. Separate the purpose before changing the bid.

## Practice before you open the simulator

Draft a campaign tree for one product: discovery, phrase/exact control, and product-targeting. Explain what data would move a term between groups.

## Decision standard

Separate intent or control levels when a combined structure prevents a clear decision.

## Key language

| Term         | Plain-language meaning                                               |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Campaign     | Budgeted container for a defined advertising objective.              |
| Ad group     | Sub-container that groups products and targets.                      |
| Target       | Keyword, product, category, or audience instruction for serving ads. |
| SP / SB / SD | Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display.         |

## Exit ticket

Write one sentence for each prompt before moving on: What is the observed evidence? What is your smallest safe action? What guardrail or review date will tell you whether the action worked?
