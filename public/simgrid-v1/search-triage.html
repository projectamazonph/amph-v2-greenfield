02

Where search terms come from

A keyword is what you bid on. A search term is what the shopper actually typed. Match type controls how loose that leash is — and the looser the leash, the more triage you'll do.

MATCH TYPEWHAT IT CATCHESWHY TERMS SHOW UPTRIAGE ROLE
AUTOWhatever Amazon's model thinks fits your listingAmazon goes fishing — close variants, loose complements, the occasional oddballBiggest source of both winners and waste — triage weekly, firmly but fairly.
BROADYour keyword + extra words, reorderings, synonyms"bamboo cutting board" also matches "large bamboo cutting board with handle"Your long-tail goldmine — harvest winners the moment they prove out.
PHRASEYour keyword with words before/after, order lockedSame as broad but tighter — fewer surprises, fewer discoveriesLighter triage. Mostly negatives and the occasional harvest.
EXACTThe keyword itself (and near-identical variants)You already chose this termNothing to harvest — manage by bid and budget instead.

The whole point of harvesting: broad and auto campaigns discover demand with a loose leash; exact campaigns exploit it with full control. Triage is the conveyor belt between the two — and you're the one running it.

03

The one number that matters: break-even ACOS

Break-even ACOS = your margin %. If every dollar of sale leaves you $0.34 after product, fees and shipping, then a 34% ACOS spends exactly that $0.34 back. Above the fence, every sale quietly costs you money — and you deserve better than that.

Worked example: a $24.99 board with all-in costs of $16.49 leaves $8.50 → 34% margin → 34% break-even ACOS. A term at 18% ACOS keeps roughly $4 of every sale in profit. A term at 48% burns $3.50 per order — "but it's getting sales" doesn't change that math, and now you know why.

04

Negative exact vs. negative phrase

Both block traffic. The difference is blast radius — and getting it wrong either leaves leaks open or cuts off good traffic by mistake.

TYPEBLOCKSUSE WHENEXAMPLE
NEG EXACTOnly this exact queryOne specific wrong-intent term. Surgical."glass cutting board" → block just that
NEG PHRASEAny query containing the wordsA rogue modifier contaminating a whole family of queries."free", "used", "repair", "wholesale", "refill", "jobs" → block the word

Rule of thumb: negate products at exact, negate modifiers at phrase. And never phrase-negative a core noun of your category — blocking "board" at phrase would hurt half your good traffic along with the junk.

05

Harvesting, step by step

  1. Graduate the term into a dedicated Exact campaign — one per product, or one per theme. Harvested terms shouldn't live with their parents.
  2. Start the bid near the term's average CPC × 1.1. You're paying for proven demand — a shy bid just starves it.
  3. Give it its own budget so a head term can't eat a long-tail winner's spend.
  4. Decide on the source: some managers negate the harvested term back out of broad/auto for clean data; others leave it and watch for overlap. Both work — pick one and stay consistent.
  5. Re-check in 7–14 days. Harvested terms can drift too — when ACOS crosses break-even, the same five friendly questions still apply.

COACH'S NOTES // TIPS

Notes from your coach.

Short, kind rules that hold up against real search term reports. Read one, try one, keep one.

COACH'S TIP ▸

HABITS TO BUILD

  • Triage weekly, on a 7-day window. A full week smooths out the noise for you — daily numbers jump around and can worry you for no reason.
  • Compare ACOS to break-even, never to a feeling. Write your margin % somewhere you can see it.
  • Harvest the misspellings. "bambo cutting board" converts like the real thing — and often costs less.
  • Keep a little swipe file of negative patterns per category. "free / used / repair / wholesale / refill" shows up everywhere.
  • Re-triage kindly after Prime Day and BFCM. Event traffic distorts everything, then settles back down.
  • Give harvested terms their own exact campaign and budget. Winners shouldn't fight their parents for spend.

TRAPS TO SKIP

  • Don't judge a term on 4 clicks. Small samples make up heroes and villains that aren't real.
  • Don't negate a converting term just because the ACOS looks scary. Above break-even means "bid down", not "goodbye".
  • Don't phrase-negative core category nouns. Blocking "bottle" at phrase hurts good traffic too.
  • Don't leave auto campaigns alone for months. They'll happily spend your Q4 budget on the word "jobs".
  • Don't harvest a head term at a long-tail bid. "cutting board" needs its own budget and an honest CPC.
  • Don't skip negatives while scaling spend. Scaling without negatives is just scaling the leak.

TOOL 06 // BID LADDER DRILL

Find the right bid, step by step.

Six rounds. I'll hand you the AOV, historical CVR and target ACOS — you tell me the highest CPC you'd pay. Within 15% counts as a hit, and the ladder shows you exactly where you landed, so every round teaches you something.

The little formula

MAX BID = AOV × CVR × TARGET ACOS

$24.99 × 10% × 25%
= $0.62 max CPC

Swap target ACOS for break-even ACOS (= margin %) and you'll find the wall — the bid where every order nets $0. We never want to bid past it.
