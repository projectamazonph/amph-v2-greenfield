## What you can do after this lesson

Explain the workflow a real operator runs to pace a Sponsored Products daily budget, name the three places pacing is set, and read the simulator's flight-log feedback before you ship a day-parting change.

## Why this workflow exists

A Sponsored Products campaign that blows its daily budget by 11 AM is not pacing. A campaign that has spent 30% of budget by 6 PM is also not pacing. Pacing means the spend fits the budget across the day in a way that lines up with the conversion pattern.

The Pacing Deck sim is the practice surface. The lesson is the workflow: read yesterday's pacing, set today's modifiers, verify at hour 6 and hour 18, and record the call.

## The decision in one sentence

Pacing is a daily rhythm. Read yesterday's curve, set today's day-parting modifier, verify at hour 6 and hour 18, record.

## What the workflow looks like end to end

A production operator who paces a real campaign runs four steps, in order:

1. **Read yesterday's pacing.** Look at the spend curve. Did the campaign hit its daily budget before peak conversion? Is the factor wrong?
2. **Set today's modifiers.** Day-parting is available where Amazon offers it. Adjust the bid modifier (up or down) on each hour-of-day or day-of-week group based on yesterday's conversion pattern.
3. **Verify at hour 6 and hour 18.** Check the spend curve at the halfway mark and again late in the day. A campaign on track at hour 6 can still blow its budget by 9 PM. A campaign under-pacing at hour 6 might still need to recover.
4. **Record.** Log the modifier set, the spend at each checkpoint, and the next review time.

Day-parting is not the same as budget cap. Day-parting is a bid modifier that turns spend up or down by hour-of-day or day-of-week. The budget cap is the daily ceiling. Both matter; they are different tools.

## How to read the simulator's feedback

Pacing Deck grades you against four signals:

- **Yesterday's curve read.** Did you spot the over- or under-spend?
- **Modifier decision.** Did the bid modifier line up with the conversion pattern?
- **Checkpoint verification.** Did you verify at hour 6 and hour 18, or did you set and forget?
- **Record.** Did you log the modifier and the spend at each checkpoint?

A high score with no checkpoint verification is a setting that might be right today and wrong tomorrow. A high score with explicit checkpoints is a pacing rhythm that survives the next handover.
