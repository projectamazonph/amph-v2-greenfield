## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can match the strength of a decision to the amount of evidence available. You will know when to watch, when to make a small change, and when to make a confident call.

## The decision in one sentence

Use the smallest safe action for a small sample, and reserve strong actions for stronger evidence.

A term gets three clicks and one order. The 33% conversion rate looks exciting. Another term gets 100 clicks and 12 orders. The second result is less dramatic, but it is a more stable signal. A single lucky sale should not decide a bid.

## Use thresholds as guardrails

A threshold is a working boundary, not a promise that a result is true. Relevance and risk still matter. A clearly wrong term may need a negative with fewer clicks, while a promotion or bid change deserves more evidence.

## Worked example

A term has 15 clicks, no orders, and a CPC above the product ceiling. The evidence supports a small review, not an automatic pause. Check relevance, compare the spend with the margin, choose one controlled bid action if the term still fits, and set a new review date.

## Your turn

A relevant keyword has 15 clicks, no orders, and a high CPC. Write the first question you would ask before lowering the bid. Then state what evidence would make you wait another week.

## Quick check

A relevant term has 3 clicks and no sales. What is the safest default?

**Answer:** Watch it. Three clicks is too small for a strong performance call. Keep the term visible, set a review date, and act sooner only if the search is clearly irrelevant or risky.

## Evidence to record

Record the click count, order count, date range, category of decision, threshold used, and next review date. The note should make sense to another VA who did not see the original report.

## Key Takeaways

- Small samples create loud but unstable signals.
- Strong actions need stronger evidence.
- Relevance and risk can override a simple click threshold.
- Every watch decision needs a date and an owner.
