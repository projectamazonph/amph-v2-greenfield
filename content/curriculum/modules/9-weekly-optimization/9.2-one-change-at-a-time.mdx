## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can design a small PPC test with one variable, one review window, and one clear success signal.

## The decision in one sentence

Change one variable at a time so the before-and-after result can teach you something.

A client asks you to improve a bamboo cutting board campaign. On Monday, you raise bids, add new keywords, increase the budget, and change the listing image. Sales rise by Friday. You have a good result but a weak explanation. You do not know which change helped.

## Why one change matters

A variable is one setting or action that can change the result. A bid, budget, keyword, negative, image, and price are separate variables. Change two at once and the result becomes harder to read.

One change is not a rule against useful work. It is a rule for learning. If an urgent stock or policy issue appears, fix it and record why it was different from a normal test.

## Worked example

Baseline: a relevant term has a ₱1.20 CPC and a 28% ACoS over the last seven days. Hypothesis: lower the bid by 15% to reduce CPC while protecting orders. Review the same term next Friday and compare spend, sales, CPC, CVR, and ACoS.

## Your turn

Design a test for a campaign with high CPC and stable conversion. Choose one variable, write the hypothesis, choose a seven-day review date, and name the signal you will watch.

## Quick check

You raise bids and add keywords on the same day. Sales rise. Which change worked?

**Answer:** You cannot tell. The test changed more than one variable, so record the result as mixed evidence and use one variable on the next run.

## Evidence to record

Create a change-log row with the date, campaign, one change, reason, baseline, review date, result, and next decision. Keep the row even when the test fails.

## Key Takeaways

- One variable creates a readable before-and-after comparison.
- A hypothesis explains what you expect to change.
- A review date prevents overnight reactions.
- The change log turns a result into team memory.

## Retrieval cue

If three settings changed together, what would you do differently on the next test?
