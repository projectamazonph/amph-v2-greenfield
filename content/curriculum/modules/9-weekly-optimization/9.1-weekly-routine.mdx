## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can run a simple weekly PPC routine without changing settings at random. You will know what to check daily, what to review weekly, and what belongs in a monthly account review.

## The decision in one sentence

Choose the smallest review that matches the signal: daily checks protect the account, weekly checks improve performance, and monthly checks protect the structure.

A VA in Cebu opens the account on Monday morning. Spend is moving, one campaign is near its budget, and a product went out of stock over the weekend. The job is not to touch every setting. The job is to find the urgent signal, record it, and route the deeper work to the right day.

## Daily, weekly, and monthly work

Daily checks catch risks that cannot wait. Look at spend, budget pacing, stock, campaign status, and accidental pauses. Do not turn a five-minute safety check into a full optimization session.

Weekly work turns evidence into controlled changes. Pull the search-term report, review winners and waste, check bids and budgets, and write a short client update.

Monthly work checks whether the account still makes sense. Review campaign structure, top keywords, listing readiness, seasonal context, and old changes that never received a verdict.

## Worked example

On Monday, a campaign is near its daily budget and the product is low in stock. The first action is a safety check: confirm the stock status and client limit, record the issue, and hold the weekly bid review until the product can fulfill orders.

## Quick check

1. A campaign is out of budget at 10 a.m. What do you check before increasing budget?
2. Which work belongs in the weekly deep pass?
3. Why should a daily check stay short?

**Answers:** Check sales, ACOS, stock, and the client limit before adding budget. Weekly work includes search-term, negative, bid, budget, and report review. A short daily check protects the account without creating random changes.

## Evidence to record

Save the weekly plan with the account name, date, owner, and the next review date. If you find an urgent issue, record the evidence that made it urgent.

## Key Takeaways

- Daily checks protect the account.
- Weekly checks turn evidence into controlled improvements.
- Monthly reviews protect structure and strategy.
- A clear routine prevents both neglect and over-tinkering.
