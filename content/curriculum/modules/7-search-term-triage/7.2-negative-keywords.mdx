## What you can do after this lesson

After this recap, you can quickly re-check a search term report against the negative-keyword rules from 2.3 and apply the right match type at the right level in your campaign.

## The decision in one sentence

Zero orders on real spend with no relevance to your product means negative it now; if a term is relevant but expensive, lower the bid instead of negating it.

## Recap: What 2.3 Already Covers

The full negative-keyword lesson lives in 2.3 (Negative Keywords: Stopping Wasted Spend). This lesson is a 5-minute check-in before you take the STR Triage tool live in 7.3.

From 2.3, the rules that matter most when triaging a search term report:

| If a search term is... | Do this |
|------------------------|---------|
| Real spend, zero sales, no relevance to your product | Negative phrase at campaign level |
| A competitor brand you can't out-convert | Negative exact |
| A pattern you'll never serve (cheap, free, used, how to, parts) | Negative phrase at campaign level |
| Relevant but expensive (good CVR conversion rate, high CPC) | Lower the bid, do not negate |
| Already served by your exact match campaign | Negative exact in the broad campaign, cross-campaign block |

## The Cross-Campaign Pattern (Worth Restating)

If your exact match campaign targets "[milk frother]" and your broad match campaign also matches "milk frother" searches, the two campaigns compete for the same click. You end up double-charged on CPC and conversion attribution. Add "milk frother" as a negative exact in the broad campaign. That stops the broad campaign from bidding on a search the exact campaign already serves.

Use this when a client asks why spend went down but sales barely moved:

> "We removed search terms that were costing you money without ever converting. Your ad budget now goes almost entirely toward shoppers who actually buy, which is why spend dropped but sales held steady."

## Quick check

1. A search term gets 45 clicks and ₱2,050 in spend with zero orders. Is it a negative candidate?
2. A term converts well but its CPC is high, pushing ACoS above target. Should you negative it?
3. Why does the cross-campaign negative belong in the broad match campaign, not the exact match campaign?

**Answers:** (1) Yes. Zero orders on real spend with no conversions is a classic negative candidate. (2) No. The term is relevant, just expensive. Lower the bid instead of negating it. (3) The exact campaign is the one you want to keep serving that search; the broad campaign is the one that should stop bidding on the same search.
