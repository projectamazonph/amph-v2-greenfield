## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can separate an eligibility problem from an attention problem before changing a bid.

## The decision in one sentence

Check whether the ad can serve, then check whether the result earns attention, before changing traffic volume.

No impressions means the ad is not showing. Low click-through rate means shoppers see it but do not choose it often. These are different problems. A higher bid cannot fix an ad that is still in review. A wider target cannot fix a weak image or an uncompetitive price.

## First checks by symptom

For no impressions, check campaign status, review state, budget, stock, and target eligibility. For low CTR, check the main image, title, price, rating, and whether the result appears for the right search. Keep the diagnosis tied to the broken stage.

## Worked example

A campaign shows no impressions and the status says In Review. The correct first action is to save the status, confirm product eligibility and budget, and wait for the review to clear. Do not double the bid while the ad cannot serve.

## Quick check

What should you check before raising the bid on an ad with no impressions?

**Answer:** Check campaign status, review state, product eligibility, budget, and stock. If the campaign cannot serve, a bid change is premature.

## Key Takeaways

- No impressions and low CTR are different symptoms.
- Eligibility comes before bid changes.
- Attention problems point to relevance and listing surfaces.
- One diagnosis should produce one safe next action.
