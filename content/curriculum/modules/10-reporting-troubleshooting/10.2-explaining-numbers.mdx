## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can translate common PPC metrics into a plain sentence that a client can understand and use.

## The decision in one sentence

Explain what the metric measures, what changed, and what question should be checked next.

Clients remember a sentence better than a spreadsheet. A client in Manila may not need the formula first. They need to know whether the ad bought attention, paid too much for a click, converted the visit, or put pressure on the whole business.

## Translate the metric before naming the lever

ACoS is ad spend divided by ad sales. CTR is clicks divided by impressions. Conversion rate is orders divided by clicks. TACoS compares ad spend with total revenue, including organic sales. Define the metric before recommending a change.

## Your turn

Explain a 0.5% CTR without using the acronym. Then explain what you would check before changing the bid.

## Quick check

A client asks, “Is 22% ACoS good?” What is the best first reply?

**Answer:** Compare it with margin, the target, and the prior period. Do not label a percentage good or bad without its business context.

## Evidence to record

Save the source period, formula, result, comparison, and next check. If you use a client sentence, keep the underlying numbers beside it.

## Key Takeaways

- Define the metric before recommending a lever.
- Compare the result with a target and a prior period.
- Use plain language that names business meaning.
- End with the next question, not a confident guess.
