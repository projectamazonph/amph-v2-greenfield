## What you can do after this lesson

Explain the workflow a listing-focused PPC operator runs in production, name the four facts the pressure test depends on, and read the simulator's grade for listing readiness before you spend real money.

## Why this workflow exists

In a real Amazon Ads account, the cheapest way to lose money is to turn on ads against a listing that cannot carry the traffic. A weak listing burns spend on clicks that never order. A strong listing makes the same clicks profitable.

The BuyBox Dojo simulates the workflow a senior PPC operator runs before turning a campaign on. The sim is the practice surface. The lesson is the workflow: research the product, pick the right match types, write the negatives that protect the launch, then put the listing under a 7-day PPC pressure test to see if it can carry traffic at the bid and budget you have.

## What the workflow looks like end to end

A production operator who runs this workflow for real does four things, in order:

1. **Research the product.** Confirm the ASIN, the price, the offer, the stock, the reviews, and the category. If any of those facts is missing, the pressure test is guesswork.
2. **Pick the match types.** Auto campaign for discovery, plus exact and phrase campaigns for the search terms you already know convert. The mix depends on the launch stage and the data you have.
3. **Write the negatives.** Start with the obvious ones for the category (wrong product, wrong use case, wrong audience). Add more after the first week of search-term data.
4. **Run a 7-day pressure test.** Spend at the daily budget the client approved. Watch impressions, clicks, CTR, CVR, and ACoS, plus the listing-side signals (review velocity, BSR, sessions). At day 7, decide whether the listing can carry the next budget step.

The decision at day 7 is one of three:

- **Scale.** The listing converts at or below target ACoS, with enough volume to justify a budget step up.
- **Hold.** The listing is fine but the data is not enough yet. Stay at the current budget and review in another 7 days.
- **Fix the listing.** Conversion is weak despite relevant traffic. Pause the budget step, hand the listing back to the client, and document the specific finding (image, price, reviews, offer).

## How to read the simulator's feedback

BuyBox Dojo scores you against four signals:

- **Research quality.** Did you confirm the four intake questions (product, objective, budget, approval) before you turned anything on?
- **Match-type mix.** Did you pick a mix that fits the launch stage?
- **Negative list.** Did you protect the launch from the obvious waste?
- **Pressure test decision.** At day 7, did you make the right Scale / Hold / Fix call given the data?

A high score with a weak listing is still a weak listing. The sim cannot fix a bad product page. What it can do is show whether you recognized the listing problem before you burned more spend.

## Key takeaway

A listing that cannot carry traffic will not be saved by better bidding. The workflow is research, match types, negatives, then a 7-day pressure test. The decision at day 7 is the one that protects the client's spend.
