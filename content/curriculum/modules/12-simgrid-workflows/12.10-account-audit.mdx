## What you can do after this lesson

Explain the workflow a real operator runs on an account audit, name the four finding categories the audit produces, and read the simulator's grade on urgency ranking before you ship a recommendation to a client.

## Why this workflow exists

An account audit can surface fifty findings in one pass. A real operator does not act on all fifty. The job is to rank them by urgency, pick the safest next action for each finding, and ship a short list to the client.

The Account Audit sim is the practice surface. The lesson is the ranking: waste first, scale second, listing third, thin data last. The order is the same on every audit, but the specific findings change with the account.

## What the workflow looks like end to end

A production operator who audits a real account runs four passes, in order:

1. **Waste.** Find the spend that is producing no orders. Pause it or lower the bid until you have a clear reason to restart. Waste is urgent because it is money leaving the account today.
2. **Scale.** Find the campaigns or terms that are working and have headroom in the budget. A winning term with a low bid is leaving conversions on the table. Scale is the second priority because it pays for the rest of the work.
3. **Listing.** Find the products whose conversion is weaker than their traffic. Listing fixes are usually not an urgent action for the ads account, but they are a clear handoff to the client.
4. **Thin data.** Flag the campaigns, terms, or products that do not have enough data to act on. Thin data is not a finding; it is a "wait" instruction with a re-check date.

Each finding gets one of four actions: stop (pause), hold (do nothing now, review later), scale (raise bid or budget), or fix (a listing change that goes to the client, not the ads account).

## How to read the simulator's feedback

Account Audit grades you against four signals:

- **Urgency rank.** Did you rank waste first and thin data last?
- **Action selection.** Did you pick stop / hold / scale / fix correctly for each finding?
- **Defense.** Did you name the data that drove each call?
- **Hand-off.** Did you separate the findings that go to the client (listing) from the findings that go to the ads account?

A high score with no defense is a long list of actions with no audit trail. A high score with a one-line defense per finding is an audit a client can read and approve.

:::callout{id="lesson-12-10-pitfall" variant="pitfall" title="Treating every finding as urgent"}
An audit that treats thin data and waste with the same urgency produces a long list that no client will read. Rank first, then act.

## Check

A strong answer ranks waste first and thin data last, picks the right stop / hold / scale / fix action for each finding, and separates the listing hand-off from the ads-account actions. A weak answer treats all findings the same.

## Key takeaway

Rank, then act. Waste first, scale second, listing third, thin data last. The audit is a short list of defended actions, not a long list of observations.
