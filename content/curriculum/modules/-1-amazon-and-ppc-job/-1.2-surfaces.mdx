## The four surfaces

You will work in four places as a PPC VA. Two are Amazon's. Two are part of this Academy.

| Surface | URL or location | Who owns it | What lives there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amazon marketplace | amazon.com (or amazon.com.ph for PH) | Amazon (the company) | Product detail pages shoppers see. |
| Seller Central | sellercentral.amazon.com | The seller (or their agency) | Listings, inventory, orders, account health, fees. |
| Amazon Ads Console | advertising.amazon.com | The seller (or their agency) | Campaigns, ad groups, keywords, bids, performance reports. |
| Project Amazon PH Academy | the platform you are using right now | AMPH | Lessons, practice tools, your portfolio, your enrollment. |

You will spend most of your time in the Amazon Ads Console. You will occasionally check Seller Central to confirm a listing is live and in stock. You will rarely touch the marketplace directly except to see what a shopper sees. You will spend the rest of your Academy time inside this platform.

## Seller Central, the seller's back office

Seller Central is the part of Amazon that the seller logs into every day. It is where they:

- Create and edit product listings (titles, descriptions, images, bullet points, A+ content).
- Manage inventory and restock decisions.
- See orders and manage shipping (for sellers using FBM instead of FBA).
- Pay Amazon fees and download invoices.
- Check account health, which is Amazon's score for how well the seller follows the rules.
- Open support cases when something is broken.

A PPC VA does not usually log into Seller Central. The seller does. You will sometimes ask the seller to confirm something in Seller Central (a listing is live, a product is in stock, a fee has changed) but you will not edit listings yourself unless the seller explicitly delegates that to you.

## Amazon Ads Console, where ads live

The Ads Console (also called Campaign Manager) is where you will spend most of your time. It looks like a separate website from Seller Central, and it is. The login uses the same email and password as Seller Central, but the screens are different.

The Ads Console is where you:

- Create campaigns, ad groups, keywords, and ads.
- Set bids and budgets.
- Pause, enable, or archive campaigns.
- Pull performance reports (impressions, clicks, spend, sales).
- Configure bidding strategies (the rules Amazon uses to adjust your bid in each auction).
- See the search-term report (the list of actual shopper searches that triggered your ads).

Two screens matter most for a beginner:

- **Campaign Manager**: the dashboard listing all campaigns, their status, and the high-level numbers for the date range you select.
- **Reports**: the section where you download bulk performance data, usually as a CSV.

You will learn the layout of these screens through the practice simulators and through the eventual real-account walkthrough. The exact buttons and labels change as Amazon updates the console, but the structure (campaign > ad group > keyword > ad) does not.

## The product detail page, where a shopper lands

When a shopper clicks a Sponsored Products ad, they land on the product detail page. This is the page the seller builds in Seller Central. A typical product detail page has:

- The main image: the first photo Amazon shows, usually a clean product photo on a white background.
- Additional images: usually 5 to 7 more photos showing the product from other angles, in use, or with size details.
- The title: a short product description. Amazon caps its length, and the cap varies by category, so the number a seller works to comes from the listing editor in Seller Central.
- The price: shown large, with strikethrough on the previous price if there is a sale.
- The buy box: the button a shopper clicks to add the product to their cart. Only one seller wins the buy box at a time.
- Bullet points: 5 short descriptions of features or benefits.
- A+ content: extra images and text below the bullets, available to brand-registered sellers.
- Reviews: star rating and recent reviews from shoppers.
- The product description: a longer text block further down the page.

The product detail page is what a click becomes. If the page is weak (bad image, thin bullets, no reviews), the shopper leaves. The best campaign in the world cannot save a weak listing.

## How the four surfaces connect

A shopper's path looks like this:

1. The shopper searches Amazon for "bamboo cutting board."
2. Amazon shows a mix of organic results and Sponsored Products. The shopper sees your ad.
3. The shopper clicks. Amazon charges you one CPC.
4. The shopper lands on the product detail page (Seller Central content).
5. The shopper decides whether to buy. If they buy, Amazon attributes the sale to your ad (if it happened within the attribution window).
6. You open the Ads Console, see the click and the sale, and decide whether the next click is worth the cost.

You will work mostly at step 6. The shopper's path is mostly invisible to you until a report surfaces it.

## Key takeaway

Four surfaces, four jobs. Marketplace is what shoppers see. Seller Central is where the seller manages listings and orders. The Ads Console is where you run ads and pull reports. This Academy is where you learn. The wrong surface is the most common beginner mistake.
