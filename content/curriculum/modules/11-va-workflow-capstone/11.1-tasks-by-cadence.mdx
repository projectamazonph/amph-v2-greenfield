## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can place PPC work on a daily, weekly, or monthly cadence and explain why it belongs there.

## The decision in one sentence

Use the shortest cadence that protects the account, and the longer cadence that gives enough evidence for a strategic decision.

A client asks why you did not rebuild the account today. The answer is simple: daily work protects spend and stock, weekly work improves search and bid decisions, and monthly work checks whether the structure still supports the business.

## Match the work to the cadence

Daily work includes spend, alerts, stock, campaign status, and accidental pauses. Weekly work includes search-term harvest, negatives, bids, budgets, and the client report. Monthly work includes structure, top keywords, listing readiness, seasonal context, and cleanup.

## Worked example

A daily alert shows that a campaign is spending quickly, a weekly report shows one irrelevant search family, and the monthly review shows two campaigns with overlapping purpose. Route the alert to today, the search-term cleanup to this week, and the structure review to the monthly plan.

## Your turn

Take five PPC tasks and assign each to daily, weekly, or monthly work. Add the owner and the evidence you expect to see after the task.

## Quick check

Where does search-term harvesting belong?

**Answer:** It belongs in the weekly deep pass. The search-term report needs a defined period and enough evidence to support a decision.

## Evidence to record

Save the cadence plan with task, owner, date, input report, output artifact, and escalation rule. Review the plan when the account changes or a client changes the objective.

## Key Takeaways

- Daily work protects the account.
- Weekly work turns evidence into controlled changes.
- Monthly work protects structure and strategy.
- Every cadence needs an owner and an output.
