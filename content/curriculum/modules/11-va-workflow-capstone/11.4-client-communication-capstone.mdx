## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can write a short client update, ask for approval with evidence, and assemble the work products that show how you think.

## The decision in one sentence

Communicate the truth, name the action, state the risk, and make the next decision easy for the client or manager.

A useful update is short, true, and forward-looking. It does not hide bad numbers or bury the client in every raw row. It says what changed, what you did, what remains uncertain, and what you recommend next.

## The capstone work products

Prepare a listing-readiness finding, a keyword plan, a campaign map, a bid guardrail, a search-term decision, a change-log row, and a weekly client update. Each artifact should show the evidence, decision, reason, owner, and next review.

Do not treat a simulator percentage as a hiring signal. The useful proof is the quality of the work and the clarity of the explanation. A reviewer should be able to follow the path from source evidence to action.

## Quick check

What should an approval request contain?

**Answer:** Include the signal, evidence checked, recommended action, risk, approval needed, owner, and review date.

## Evidence to record

Save the capstone artifacts together with their source reports, dates, version, owner, and review note. Keep simulator results as formative feedback, not as proof of employment readiness.

## Key Takeaways

- A client update is short, true, and forward-looking.
- Good communication connects evidence to action and risk.
- The capstone is a portfolio of decisions, not a single score.
- Every artifact needs an owner and a review date.

## Retrieval cue

Can a reviewer trace your final recommendation back to the evidence that caused it?
