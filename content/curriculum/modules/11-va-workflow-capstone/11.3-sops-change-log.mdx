## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can turn a repeated PPC task into a short standard operating procedure and leave an audit trail for every change.

## The decision in one sentence

Write the next person’s steps clearly enough that they can repeat the work, check the evidence, and know when to escalate.

A change log is your memory, your proof, and your teacher. Without it, a team cannot tell whether a bid changed because of a rule, a client request, or a guess. A short SOP keeps the same decision from being reinvented every week.

## Build an SOP around a decision

Name the task. State when it starts. List the input report or screen. Give the steps in order. Define the safe boundary. State what evidence to save. End with the escalation rule and review date.

## Your turn

Write a four-step SOP for weekly search-term review. Include the report date range, sorting rule, decision categories, and change-log fields.

## Quick check

What makes a change log useful to another VA?

**Answer:** It shows the date, scope, change, evidence, owner, result, and next review. Another VA can understand the decision without guessing.

## Evidence to record

Save the SOP version, change-log row, source report, owner, and review date. When a rule changes, record what changed and why.

## Key Takeaways

- An SOP turns judgment into repeatable steps.
- A change log preserves the reason behind the action.
- Safe boundaries and escalation rules belong in the SOP.
- Good records help the next person learn from the result.
