## What you can do after this lesson

After this lesson, you can separate routine VA actions from changes that need manager approval.

## The decision in one sentence

Do the small, rule-based work you are authorized to do, and stop for approval before a large or strategic change.

A manager asks you to pause a whole campaign because sales fell today. You should not pause first. Check stock, price, reviews, and competitors. Then bring the evidence and ask for approval because a whole-campaign pause is a large lever.

## Use the permissions ladder

Routine work can include downloading reports, cleaning data, adding negatives from approved rules, making small bid moves, and promoting a proven term when the SOP allows it. Budget changes, new campaign structures, whole-campaign pauses, strategy shifts, and client-facing commitments need approval unless your written permission says otherwise.

## Your turn

Classify these actions as independent or approval-required: add a negative from a written rule, change the daily budget, download a report, pause a whole campaign, and move a proven term to Exact. State the evidence you would attach to one approval request.

## Quick check

A manager asks for a whole-campaign pause after one bad day. What is the safe response?

**Answer:** Check stock, price, reviews, and competitors first. Bring the evidence and ask for approval before making a large change.

## Evidence to record

Save the request, permission rule, checks completed, recommendation, approval decision, owner, and review date. Never use a private message as the only record for a major account change.

## Key Takeaways

- Permission is about scope and risk, not confidence.
- Small rule-based actions can be independent when authorized.
- Large, strategic, or client-facing changes need approval.
- A good approval note makes the decision easy to review.
