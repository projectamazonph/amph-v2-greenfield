## What the workflow looks like end to end

The capstone sequence is six stages. The order is the same on every engagement; the specific actions change with the account and the data.

1. **Onboard.** Confirm access, KPI, product facts, approval rules, and launch blockers. The handoff has to be right before anything else can be right.
2. **Research and structure.** Seed the keyword list, expand it, score it, group it. Build the campaign structure with one campaign per intent cluster, the right match types, the bid defaults that respect your maximum CPC, and the negative list that protects the launch.
3. **Launch.** Open the auto campaign at the discovery budget. Open the exact and phrase campaigns at the bid default. Set the pacing modifiers. Record the launch date and the day-7 review date.
4. **Optimize.** Read the report. Triage the search terms. Adjust the bids. Verify the pacing. Log every change with the expected signal and the next review time.
5. **Audit.** Run the four-pass account audit. Waste first, scale second, listing third, thin data last. Hand the listing fixes back to the client.
6. **Report.** Write the client report. Numbers, what changed, what is next. Close the loop and queue the next review.

At the end of the capstone, you should be able to point to a documented decision for each stage: research sources cited, structure choices defended, bid changes logged, audit findings ranked, report delivered.

## How to read the simulator's feedback

The capstone sequence grades you against the integration, not the individual pieces:

- **Order.** Did you run the six stages in the right order?
- **Defensibility.** Did you record the decision at each stage with the signal that drove it?
- **Approval.** Did you stay inside the approval rules defined at onboarding?
- **Audit trail.** Did the next operator find the reasoning at each step?

A high score with no record at one stage is a workflow that depends on you being there to defend it. A high score with a record at every stage is a workflow that survives you leaving.
