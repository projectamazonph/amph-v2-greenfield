## What you can do after this lesson

Calculate ROAS (return on ad spend), convert between ROAS and ACoS (advertising cost of sales), and know when each framing is the useful one.

## The decision in one sentence

Use ACoS when you're optimizing a campaign yourself; use ROAS when you're explaining the same result to someone who thinks in returns, not costs.

You already know ACoS, the percentage of ad revenue that went to ad costs. ROAS is the same relationship, flipped: instead of "what percent of my revenue went to ads," ROAS asks "for every peso I spend on ads, how many pesos come back."

## The simple math

**Formula:** `ROAS = ad sales ÷ ad spend`

| Ad spend | Ad sales | ROAS | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₱100 | ₱200 | 2.0x | every ₱1 spent earned ₱2 |
| ₱100 | ₱300 | 3.0x | every ₱1 spent earned ₱3 |
| ₱100 | ₱400 | 4.0x | every ₱1 spent earned ₱4 |
| ₱100 | ₱500 | 5.0x | every ₱1 spent earned ₱5 |

Higher ROAS means more revenue per ad peso.

## ROAS vs ACoS: same relationship, two views

`ROAS = 1 ÷ ACoS` and `ACoS = 1 ÷ ROAS`.

| ACoS | ROAS | Profitable at a 35% margin? |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 5.0x | Yes, very profitable |
| 25% | 4.0x | Yes, healthy |
| 33% | 3.0x | Yes, still profitable |
| 40% | 2.5x | No, above the 35% margin |
| 50% | 2.0x | No, losing money |

**Use ACoS** when you're optimizing campaigns day to day. "Lower ACoS" is a direct, actionable target.

**Use ROAS** when you're talking to a client or business owner who isn't fluent in PPC. "For every peso you put into ads, you get ₱3.50 back" reads as a return, which is usually the frame a business owner already thinks in.

## When to target a specific ROAS

Just like ACoS has a break-even point, ROAS has a minimum threshold: `minimum ROAS = 1 ÷ profit margin`.

For a product with a 35% margin: minimum ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.35 = **2.86x**. Anything below that loses money on ads.

For a product with a 50% margin: minimum ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.50 = **2.0x**. There's more room here, even 2.5x is profitable.

Target ROAS (the number you aim for) should sit above the minimum, to leave a safety cushion:

| Margin | Minimum ROAS | Target ROAS with cushion |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | 3.33x | 4.0x+ |
| 35% | 2.86x | 3.5x+ |
| 40% | 2.50x | 3.0x+ |
| 50% | 2.00x | 2.5x+ |

Three bands worth recognizing by reflex:

:::trade-off{id="roas-bands" title="Reading ROAS bands"}

| ROAS band | What it means                                                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| ROAS 1    | Break even on ad spend (no profit, no loss)                        |
| ROAS 3    | A common short-term target for new products                        |
| ROAS 5+   | Strong return, usually signals a winner                             |

:::

:::callout{id="roas-not-profit" variant="warning" title="ROAS is not profit"}
ROAS returns ad sales, not net profit. A ROAS of 5 with a 30% cost of goods still leaves you with margins to check.
:::

## Worked example: two campaigns compared

Same product, a ₱1,750 yoga block with a 40% margin.

**Campaign A:** ₱20,000 spend, 350 clicks, 45 orders, ₱78,750 ad sales, ₱57 CPC, 12.9% CVR, 25.4% ACoS, 3.94x ROAS.

**Campaign B:** ₱10,000 spend, 180 clicks, 20 orders, ₱35,000 ad sales, ₱56 CPC, 11.1% CVR, 28.6% ACoS, 3.50x ROAS.

Campaign A has both the lower ACoS (25.4% vs 28.6%) and the higher ROAS (3.94x vs 3.50x), while Campaign B is slightly more efficient per click on CPC. But Campaign A generates more than double the revenue (₱78,750 vs ₱35,000) while staying profitable.

Don't optimize for the highest ROAS in isolation. A 5.0x ROAS on ₱2,500 in ad spend (₱12,500 in ad sales) is worth less than a 3.5x ROAS on ₱50,000 in ad spend (₱175,000 in ad sales). Profitable volume beats a small edge in efficiency at a much smaller scale.

## Quick check

1. You spend ₱15,000 and generate ₱45,000 in ad sales. What's your ROAS?
2. Your product's margin is 42%. What's the minimum acceptable ROAS?
3. If ROAS drops from 4.0x to 2.5x, what happened to ACoS?

**Answers:** (1) ROAS = ₱45,000 ÷ ₱15,000 = 3.0x. (2) Minimum ROAS = 1 ÷ 0.42 = 2.38x, so 3.0x from question 1 would be profitable on this product. (3) ACoS went from 25% (1 ÷ 4.0) to 40% (1 ÷ 2.5), a meaningful jump that could push the campaign above margin depending on the product.

## Client language

Use this when reporting results to a business owner rather than a PPC colleague:

> "For every peso you put into this campaign, you're getting back ₱3.50 in ad-attributed sales. That's a 3.5x return, and it's above the 2.9x we need to stay profitable on this product's margin."

## Key takeaways

- ROAS = ad sales ÷ ad spend, the revenue return per ad peso.
- ROAS and ACoS are mathematical inverses: the same underlying data, framed differently.
- Use ROAS with stakeholders, ACoS when optimizing campaigns yourself.
- Calculate the minimum ROAS (1 ÷ margin) and target above it, not just above zero.
- Optimize for profitable revenue, not the single highest ROAS number.
