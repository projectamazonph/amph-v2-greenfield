## What you can do after this lesson

Read the six core Amazon pay-per-click (PPC) metrics as one diagnostic system, identify the likely performance problem, and choose the first question to investigate before changing anything.

## The decision in one sentence

Don't change a bid because one metric looks bad. First find out whether the issue is traffic, conversion, cost, budget, or the campaign's own objective.

The path matters because each handoff answers a different operational question. A campaign can have plenty of impressions but weak traffic appeal, strong traffic but weak listing conversion, or healthy conversion with unaffordable click costs. The visible symptom is not automatically the correct lever.

## Meet the Big Six

The Big Six are the calculated metrics that describe what happened between the search result and the sale. Each metric has a job. Treating them as interchangeable is how operators cut bids before they know what broke.

:::visual{id="big-six-matrix" kind="metric-matrix" title="The Big Six answer different questions"}
{"metrics":[{"label":"CPC","family":"traffic","question":"How much does one click cost?","formula":"ad spend ÷ clicks","lever":"bid and placement pressure"},{"label":"CTR","family":"traffic","question":"Does the result earn attention?","formula":"clicks ÷ impressions","lever":"query relevance, offer, image, title"},{"label":"CVR","family":"conversion","question":"Does the traffic become orders?","formula":"orders ÷ clicks","lever":"listing readiness and intent fit"},{"label":"ACoS","family":"economics","question":"What share of ad sales is spent?","formula":"ad spend ÷ ad sales","lever":"objective, CPC, conversion"},{"label":"ROAS","family":"economics","question":"How much sales return per peso?","formula":"ad sales ÷ ad spend","lever":"efficiency target and scale"},{"label":"TACoS","family":"economics","question":"What is the ad burden on the whole store?","formula":"ad spend ÷ total sales","lever":"total-store growth and mix"}]}
:::

Two more numbers feed these calculations without being part of the Big Six themselves: **impressions** (how often Amazon showed the ad) and **clicks** (how often a shopper selected it). CPC and CTR come straight from ad platform reports. TACoS is different: it needs total sales, including organic sales that happen with no ad involved, so it's a business-level number you calculate yourself, not a line in a standard ad report.

## Start with one case

Imagine you support a client selling a stainless-steel coffee grinder for $40.

This week, one campaign produced:

- 20,000 impressions.
- 160 clicks.
- $192 ad spend.
- 8 orders.
- $320 ad-attributed sales.
- $800 total sales.

Now calculate the metrics before reading the visual readout.

The ACoS is high. That's a signal. It isn't the decision yet. The case shows why: ACoS tells you the size of the efficiency gap, but not whether the first fix belongs to traffic, conversion, click cost, or campaign strategy.

## Ask the questions in order

### 1. What is the campaign trying to do?

Check the objective before you judge the result. A launch campaign may accept a higher ACoS while it gathers search-term data. A mature exact-match campaign may need to stay close to a profitability target. A branded-defense campaign is measured differently again.

### 2. Is there enough data?

Eight orders give more information than zero orders, but they don't prove a trend will continue. Check the date range, spend, clicks, seasonality, price changes, stock, and recent campaign changes. Don't make a large change because of one noisy day.

### 3. Is traffic the problem?

The campaign received many impressions, but only 0.8% of shoppers clicked. Start with traffic relevance and search-result appeal. Check the actual search terms, target breadth, main image, visible product promise, price, coupon, shipping, featured-offer position, and stock.

### 4. Is conversion the problem?

The campaign produced 160 clicks and 8 orders. A 5% conversion rate may be acceptable or weak depending on category, price, traffic intent, and account history. Compare it with the product's normal conversion rate and similar campaign traffic. Inspect the title, images, price, reviews, variations, stock, and delivery promise before calling the keyword bad.

### 5. Is the click cost affordable?

If the target ACoS is 30% and this campaign is at 60%, the cost per order is likely too high, conversion is too low, or both. Use maximum CPC to make the bid decision defensible:

`maximum CPC = selling price × target ACoS × conversion rate`

For this case, with a $40 price, 30% target ACoS, and 5% conversion rate:

`$40 × 0.30 × 0.05 = $0.60`

The maximum CPC is about **$0.60**. The actual CPC is $1.20. The campaign pays about twice the CPC this combination can support. Your next action could be to lower bids, reduce placement pressure, focus on more relevant targets, or improve conversion. The correct choice depends on what you found in the earlier questions, not on this number alone.

## Client language

Use this structure when you explain a performance issue:

:::visual{id="recommendation-builder" kind="recommendation-builder" title="Explain the evidence, not the emotion"}
{"sentence":"|Target: current ACoS is above the supplied target|Pattern: clicks cost more than current conversion can support|Next check: search terms and listing conversion|Action boundary: no large bid reduction before evidence|Review date: document the change and inspect the next data window|"}
:::

> "This campaign spent more than the current target because clicks cost more than the product's current conversion rate can support. I'm checking the search terms and listing conversion before making a bid reduction. I'll document the action and review the result after the next data window."

## Key takeaway

Metrics are a diagnostic system, not a scoreboard. Read the path from impression to order, confirm the objective, and choose the smallest safe action that fits the evidence.
