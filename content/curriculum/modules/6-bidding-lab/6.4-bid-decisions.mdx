## What you can do after this lesson

Explain the workflow a real operator runs before touching a bid, name the six numbers the decision depends on, and read the simulator's grade on raise / hold / lower / investigate moves before you ship a bid change.

## Why this workflow exists

A bid change is the cheapest change to make and the easiest one to regret. Raise on a term that did not have it, and the bid falls past zero at the next weekly review. Lower on a term that was just learning, and you kill a winner before its first 30 clicks.

The Bid Decisions sim is the practice surface. The lesson is the read: six numbers in front of you, four moves available, one of them correct.

## The decision in one sentence

Read six numbers, pick one of four moves, change the bid in a small defensible step. The read is the work; the bid change follows.

## What the workflow looks like end to end

A production operator who decides on a real bid reads six numbers first:

1. **Clicks.** How many real auctions has this term been in?
2. **Spend.** What did the term cost across those clicks?
3. **Sales.** What did the term produce in attributed revenue?
4. **ACoS.** Spend divided by sales, for this term.
5. **ROAS.** Sales divided by spend, for this term.
6. **Confidence.** How many clicks does it have? A term with 8 clicks and 1 order is not a winner; it is noise.

Then pick one of four moves:

- **Raise.** Confidence is high, performance is above target, and the term has headroom in the budget. Raise in a small step (5-10%), not in a jump.
- **Hold.** Performance is on target, and a change would risk breaking the data. Document the hold and the next review date.
- **Lower.** Confidence is high, performance is below target, and the term is consuming spend that could go to winners. Lower in a small step, then review at the next observation window.
- **Investigate.** Confidence is high and the signal is mixed (high CTR, weak CVR; or low CTR, good CVR). Do not change the bid until the underlying question is answered.

## Worked example

The workflow above is the walkthrough a real operator runs in production. Step through it once on the sim, name each step in your own words, and record the deliverable you would hand to the client.

## How to read the simulator's feedback

Bid Decisions grades you against four signals:

- **Read depth.** Did you read all six numbers, or did you act on one?
- **Move selection.** Did you pick raise / hold / lower / investigate based on the data?
- **Confidence handling.** Did you act on a low-confidence signal, or did you flag it as noise?
- **Step size.** Did you change the bid in a defensible step, or did you make a jump?

A high score with a jump-sized change is a change you will regret. A high score with a small, defensible step is a change that compounds.
