## What you can do after this lesson

Explain what an Amazon PPC virtual assistant does, name the three course stages ahead of you, and describe the work loop you'll use in every lesson and tool.

## Who you are in this system

You are learning to be an Amazon PPC virtual assistant. The job is real and the market is real: thousands of Amazon sellers, mostly small and mid-sized brands, hire remote assistants to run their advertising. Many of them hire in the Philippines because of the time-zone overlap with the US, the English fluency, and the value.

What you will actually do for a client:

- Open their Amazon Ads account and read the previous week's numbers.
- Decide which campaigns, keywords, or bids need attention.
- Make the approved change, or send the change to the client for approval.
- Write a one-paragraph update that says what changed, why, and when you will check the result again.

You will not:

- Touch the client's product page design.
- Place orders or change prices.
- Decide the client's business objective. The client decides what success means. You decide whether the campaign is on track to hit that target.

## What Amazon actually is

Amazon started as an online bookstore in 1994 and is now one of the largest marketplaces in the world. Most of the products sold on Amazon are not sold by Amazon itself. They are sold by independent sellers who use Amazon's website to reach shoppers. Amazon handles the payment, the customer service, and (for many sellers) the shipping and storage through a service called FBA, Fulfillment by Amazon. Sellers pay Amazon a referral fee (a percentage of each sale) and, if they use FBA, a fulfillment fee.

Two surfaces you will use:

- Seller Central, the seller-side platform at `sellercentral.amazon.com`, where the seller manages the product listings, inventory, orders, and account health.
- Amazon Ads Console (also called Campaign Manager), at `advertising.amazon.com`, where ads are created, edited, and reported. This is where you will spend most of your time.

A shopper searching for "bamboo cutting board" sees two kinds of results:

- Organic results: products ranked by Amazon's search algorithm. Sellers do not pay per click on these.
- Sponsored results: products where the seller paid to appear. Sponsored Products look like organic results but carry a small "Sponsored" label under the title. This is where PPC lives.

PPC stands for Pay-Per-Click. The seller pays only when a shopper clicks the ad, not when Amazon shows it.

## A vocabulary snapshot

You will see these terms across the next few lessons. Each gets a full definition when you reach it, and the full glossary is always one click away.

- Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display: the three ad types. Most beginners start with Sponsored Products.
- ACoS, TACoS, ROAS, CPC, CTR, conversion rate: the six metrics you will learn to read together. They are defined in Lessons 1.1 to 1.4.
- ASIN: Amazon Standard Identification Number. Every product listing has one. It looks like B07PXGQC41.
- FBA vs FBM: Fulfillment by Amazon vs Fulfillment by Merchant. Most ads work is for FBA sellers, where Amazon ships the order.
- Search term: the actual words a shopper typed, as opposed to the keyword the seller targeted. These can differ.

Before you open an ad console, decide which facts you need to read, which change is safe to recommend, and how you will explain the reason to the client.

## The job in one sentence

An Amazon PPC VA reads account data, makes a defensible advertising decision, makes the approved change, and explains it in plain English.

Amazon PPC (Pay-Per-Click) is Amazon's Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display advertising system. A seller pays each time a shopper clicks their ad, not each time it's shown. Your job is running that system on a client's behalf, safely.

## This is not a memorization course

Amazon PPC has formulas, reports, settings, and acronyms. You'll learn them. The real skill is knowing what to do when the numbers change.

For example, a campaign can have a high ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sales, ad spend divided by ad sales) for different reasons:

- The campaign bought clicks from the wrong searches.
- The product page didn't convince shoppers to buy.
- The keyword converts, but the cost per click is too high.
- The campaign is in a planned launch phase and the client accepts a higher cost for now.

The number alone doesn't tell you which one it is. You need a work loop.

## Your work loop

- **Read.** Check the objective and the data.
- **Decide.** Identify the likely problem and the safest next step.
- **Change.** Make the approved update in the account, or in one of this platform's tools while you're still practicing.
- **Explain.** Record what changed, why it changed, and when you'll review it.

This loop is what separates a VA who guesses from one a client keeps on retainer. It also makes handovers easier when another VA, a manager, or the client needs to understand your decision later.

## Your first professional habit

Don't guess when information is missing.

Before you touch a PPC task, ask for the facts that control the decision:

- Product or ASIN.
- Objective.
- Budget.
- Target ACoS, or the profitability rule the client uses.
- Current stock position.
- Price, offer, and promotion plans.
- Any approved limits on bids, budgets, or campaign changes.

A good question protects a client's account. A fast, unsupported change can cost real money. Lesson 0.3 walks through this in detail with a sample client brief.

## Client language

Use this when you need information before starting work:

> "Before I build the campaign, please confirm the product goal, daily budget, target ACoS, and whether the listing is in stock and ready to advertise. This helps me set safe bids and measure the result correctly."
