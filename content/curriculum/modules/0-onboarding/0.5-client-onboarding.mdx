## What you can do after this lesson

Explain the workflow a real operator runs on a client handoff, name the five questions that have to be answered before work starts, and read the simulator's grade on access, KPI, facts, approval rules, and launch blockers before you touch the account.

## Why this workflow exists

A client handoff that does not name the rights, the rules, and the launch blockers is a handoff that ends in a lost weekend. A PPC operator who starts work without these five answers is a PPC operator who will guess the rest of the account.

The Client Onboarding sim is the practice surface. The lesson is the handoff checklist. Every new client starts with the same five questions, even the ones who say "just turn on ads."

## The decision in one sentence

Five questions, five answers, one approval. The handoff doc is the audit trail. Do not turn on ads before the doc is signed.

## What the workflow looks like end to end

A production operator who onboards a real account for the first time runs five steps, in order:

1. **Access.** Confirm Seller Central delegated access and Amazon Ads console access with the right permission level. A read-only access is not enough for an ads operator. Document the login, the MFA, and the person to escalate to if the access breaks.
2. **KPI.** Confirm the objective and the success metric. Is the client optimizing for ACoS, TACoS, ROAS, sales volume, or something else? "Make it work" is not a KPI.
3. **Product facts.** Confirm the ASIN list, the price, the stock, the offer, and any planned promotions. The launch calendar drives the campaign calendar.
4. **Approval rules.** Confirm what you can change without asking (current plan = routine bid and negative changes) and what requires approval (budget step ups, structure changes, anything that changes spend by more than a small threshold).
5. **Launch blockers.** Flag anything that prevents launch: missing access, no budget, no approved offer, listing in stock issue, missing KPI. Do not turn anything on while a blocker is open.

The output is a one-page onboarding doc that the client signs (or replies "approved" to). The doc is the audit trail that protects both sides if the work goes wrong.

## Worked example

The workflow above is the walkthrough a real operator runs in production. Step through it once on the sim, name each step in your own words, and record the deliverable you would hand to the client.

## How to read the simulator's feedback

Client Onboarding grades you against five signals:

- **Access.** Did you confirm access at the right permission level?
- **KPI.** Did you name a success metric, not "make it work"?
- **Facts.** Did you confirm ASIN, price, stock, offer, calendar?
- **Approval rules.** Did you separate routine from approval-required?
- **Launch blockers.** Did you flag anything that prevents a safe launch?

A high score with no approval rules is a handoff that produces "I thought you said yes" later. A high score with explicit rules is a handoff that survives the first disagreement.
