## What you can do after this lesson

Explain the operator surface AdConsole Pro emulates, name the four navigation layers a real Sponsored Ads operator works in, and read the simulator's pacing and auction feedback before you touch a bid or budget.

## Why this workflow exists

A real Amazon Ads operator spends most of their day inside Campaign Manager. They move between four layers: the campaigns list, the ad group, the keyword or product targeting list, and the search-term report. Each layer answers a different question.

The AdConsole Pro sim is the practice surface. The lesson is the navigation logic: which layer you open depends on what you are trying to protect or improve, and the same account will look different at 9 AM, at noon, and at 6 PM.

## The decision in one sentence

Open the campaigns layer first for pacing, the ad group layer for diagnosis, the keyword and search-term layers for action, and the auction layer for placement strategy.

## What the workflow looks like end to end

A production operator who runs this surface for real does four things, in order:

1. **Open the campaigns layer.** Read yesterday's pacing, account-level ACoS, TACoS, and the day's spend rate. Flag campaigns that are pacing too fast or too slow against the daily budget.
2. **Drill into the ad group.** Compare each ad group's performance against its own brief. A healthy campaign at the account level can still hide a single ad group that is bleeding spend.
3. **Read the keyword and search-term layers.** Sort by spend, then by ACoS. Identify the search terms that need a bid change, a pause, or a negative match.
4. **Check the auction layer.** Look at placement modifiers, hour-of-day, and impression share. A bid that wins cheap placements does not always win the placement that converts.

The pacing decision is hourly in a launch week. Outside launch week, the same decisions happen on a daily or weekly cadence, depending on spend volume.

## Worked example

The workflow above is the walkthrough a real operator runs in production. Step through it once on the sim, name each step in your own words, and record the deliverable you would hand to the client.

## How to read the simulator's feedback

AdConsole Pro scores you against four signals:

- **Layer choice.** Did you open the right layer first for the question you were trying to answer?
- **Pacing read.** Did you spot the campaigns that were over- or under-pacing against the daily budget?
- **Search-term action.** Did you pick the right keep / negate / pause action for the term?
- **Auction interpretation.** Did you distinguish a placement problem from a relevance problem?

A high score means your navigation matched the question you were trying to answer. A low score usually means you opened the keyword layer when the budget layer had the answer.
