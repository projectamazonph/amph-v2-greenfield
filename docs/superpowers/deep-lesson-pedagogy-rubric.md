# Deep Lesson Pedagogical Review Rubric

Each lesson is reviewed as a sequence of decisions rather than as a collection of components. A visual block earns instructional value when it makes an otherwise invisible relationship inspectable, reduces unnecessary search, or gives the learner a constrained decision to practice.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sequence alignment** | Visuals are decorative or disconnected from the lesson decision. | Visuals appear near the relevant concept but the handoff is implicit. | Each visual advances the same decision from concept to example to practice. |
| **Cognitive load** | Too many simultaneous variables or unexplained labels. | Mostly clear, but some visual density or jargon remains. | One dominant question per visual, progressive disclosure, and clear emphasis. |
| **Evidence use** | Claims or actions are unsupported by metrics, constraints, or a data source. | Evidence is named but not consistently tied to the next move. | Every branch or recommendation names the evidence required to justify it. |
| **Active attempt** | Learner only reads a completed answer. | Learner has a prompt but little decision consequence. | Learner chooses, classifies, calculates, or sequences before feedback. |
| **Practice handoff** | Visual ends without a usable learner artifact or next action. | Practice exists elsewhere but the bridge is weak. | Visual explicitly hands the learner into the worksheet, simulator, or retrieval check. |
| **Feedback quality** | No explanation of why an answer is correct or risky. | Feedback names the answer but not the governing principle. | Feedback explains the principle, trade-off, and next evidence check. |

A lesson can score up to 12 points. Scores of 10–12 are **strong**, 7–9 are **usable but revise**, and 0–6 are **not yet pedagogically reliable**, regardless of visual-block count. The review also records a dominant failure mode: decorative, overloaded, detached, premature answer, or missing feedback.
